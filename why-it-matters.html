Protecting South Auckland's Vegetation
Issue Why Where Action

Why it matters

Vegetation is natural infrastructure

Trees, wetlands, shrubs, and riparian planting help clean water, reduce erosion, absorb carbon dioxide, provide shade, support wildlife, and lower flood risk.

When vegetation is removed, the impacts spread

Vegetation loss is not only an environmental issue. It affects waterways, soil stability, climate resilience, native species, and the health of people living in built-up areas.

Why this matters for South Auckland communities

Vegetation loss affects people as well as ecosystems. Low-lying South Auckland communities can face greater flooding risk when rainfall runs quickly off roads, roofs, and paved surfaces instead of soaking into vegetated ground.

If vegetation decline continues, South Auckland will face greater environmental damage, more pressure on native species, and increased risks from climate-related hazards.

Healthy vegetation protects communities before a disaster happens.
