The issue

The hidden cost of urban growth

Vegetation decline happens when native trees, wetlands, shrubs, and connected green corridors are removed, reduced, or broken into smaller fragments. In South Auckland, pressure comes from rapid development, land clearance, transport upgrades, and new infrastructure built across places that were once more open and vegetated.

The problem is not only losing individual trees. It is losing the living network that filters water, shelters birds and insects, stores carbon, stabilises soil, cools streets, and gives communities access to healthy green spaces.

Why we chose this issue

Local action matters because this is our place

South Auckland is not an abstract case study. It is where people live, study, travel, and build futures. When green spaces disappear, future generations inherit hotter streets, weaker ecosystems, and fewer places to connect with nature.

Choosing this issue means looking closely at our own region and asking how growth can happen without sacrificing the native environments that make Tāmaki Makaurau healthier and more resilient.

"Restoration begins when a community decides that streams, trees, and native species are not leftovers. They are part of the future."

Native species often used in restoration

  • Mānuka and kānuka for hardy early shelter
  • Harakeke near wetlands and stream edges
  • Tī kōuka for wet ground and bird habitat
  • Karamū and māhoe for native revegetation
  • Carex and toetoe for riparian planting
